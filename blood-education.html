BLOOD GROUPS

The 8 Common ABO & Rh Blood Groups

Blood groups are commonly described using the ABO and Rh systems.

A+

A Positive

One of the common ABO/Rh blood-group classifications.

A−

A Negative

An A blood group without the Rh D antigen.

B+

B Positive

A B blood group with the Rh D antigen.

B−

B Negative

A B blood group without the Rh D antigen.

AB+

AB Positive

An AB blood group with the Rh D antigen.

AB−

AB Negative

An AB blood group without the Rh D antigen.

O+

O Positive

An O blood group with the Rh D antigen.

O−

O Negative

An O blood group without the Rh D antigen.

BLOOD COMPONENTS

Blood Is More Than One Resource

Blood can be separated into components that may serve different clinical purposes.

WB

Whole Blood

Contains the major cellular and liquid components of blood together.

RBC

Packed Red Cells

A red-cell component used in clinical settings according to patient need.

FFP

Fresh Frozen Plasma

A plasma component containing important proteins and clotting factors.

PLT

Platelets

A blood component involved in normal blood clotting.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main blood groups?

The commonly referenced ABO/Rh combinations are A+, A−, B+, B−, AB+, AB−, O+ and O−.

What is a blood component?

Blood can be separated into components such as red cells, plasma and platelets. The appropriate component depends on clinical need.

Does RaktSathi guarantee blood availability?

No. RaktSathi is a coordination and discovery prototype. Availability information must be verified with the relevant authorized blood centre.

Does RaktSathi decide medical compatibility?

No. Medical compatibility and transfusion decisions are handled by qualified healthcare professionals and authorized facilities.

Can this page replace medical advice?

No. This page provides general educational information and should not replace professional medical advice or facility-level guidance.

Educational Information Only

This module is intended for general awareness. It does not diagnose medical conditions, prescribe treatment, determine transfusion compatibility or confirm blood availability. For an actual medical requirement, contact an authorized healthcare facility or qualified professional.
