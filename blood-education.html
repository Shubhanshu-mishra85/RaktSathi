BLOOD EDUCATION & SAFETY

Understand Blood.
Donate Safely.

A simple educational guide explaining how blood donation works, what happens after collection, how transfusion is performed and what to do if a possible reaction is noticed.

01 • BLOOD DONATION

How does blood donation happen?

Blood donation is performed under the supervision of trained healthcare professionals at an authorized blood centre.

1

Donor Screening

The blood centre checks the donor's health information and performs the required screening to determine whether the person is eligible to donate.

2

Blood Collection

If the donor is eligible, trained staff collect blood using sterile, single-use equipment and follow the centre's approved procedures.

3

Post-Donation Care

After donation, the donor is observed and given appropriate after-care instructions, including rest and refreshments according to the centre's protocol.

4

Safe Handling

The collected blood is identified and handled by the authorized blood centre for required testing, processing and storage.

Screening → Collection → Testing → Processing → Storage
02 • AFTER DONATION

What happens to donated blood?

Blood centres follow controlled procedures before blood or blood components can be made available for clinical use.

Identification

The donation is properly identified and recorded.

Testing

Required laboratory testing is performed according to applicable procedures.

Processing

Blood may be prepared into components such as red cells, plasma or platelets.

Storage & Issue

Components are stored under controlled conditions and issued through authorized clinical processes.

Important: VitalLoop does not collect, test, process, store, transport or issue blood. These activities are performed by authorized blood centres and healthcare professionals.
03 • BLOOD TRANSFUSION

How does a blood transfusion work?

The clinical team follows identification, compatibility and monitoring procedures before and during transfusion.

1

Patient Identification

Healthcare staff verify the patient's identity and the transfusion requirement according to hospital procedures.

2

Compatibility Checks

The healthcare team performs the required blood-group and compatibility checks before transfusion.

3

Transfusion

The blood component is administered by trained healthcare staff according to the patient's clinical requirement.

4

Monitoring

The patient is monitored during and after transfusion for any concerning signs or changes in condition.

04 • SAFETY ALERT

What if a possible transfusion reaction occurs?

If concerning symptoms appear during or after a transfusion, the healthcare team should be informed immediately.

🚨 1. Tell the healthcare staff immediately

Inform the nurse, doctor or treating healthcare professional about any sudden or concerning change.

⚠️ 2. Do not try to manage it yourself

Do not change the blood line, medicines or transfusion equipment yourself. Let trained medical staff handle it.

🩺 3. The medical team assesses the patient

Healthcare professionals follow their transfusion-reaction protocol and decide the appropriate clinical action.

📋 4. Further checks may be required

The clinical team may investigate the reaction and coordinate with the relevant blood centre as required.

🚨 Warning signs can include: Fever or chills, rash, breathing difficulty, chest discomfort, dizziness or a sudden change in the patient's condition.

If this happens during a transfusion, alert healthcare staff immediately.
MYTH VS FACT

Know the difference

MYTH

Blood can be transferred directly from one person to another.

Clinical blood collection, testing, compatibility and transfusion must follow authorized medical procedures.

MYTH

A matching blood group alone guarantees compatibility.

Healthcare professionals perform the required compatibility checks before transfusion.

FACT

VitalLoop connects people with information and resources.

VitalLoop is a discovery and coordination layer and does not replace authorized blood centres or medical professionals.
